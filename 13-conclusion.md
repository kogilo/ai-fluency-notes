## A Closer Look at Diligence

- **Diligence** is about **taking responsibility for your AI interactions**. It focuses mostly on the **ethical and safety** parts of AI fluency (*the other competencies focus mainly on effectiveness and efficiency*).
- It ensures AI use is **rigorous, transparent, and accountable**, especially in **professional environments**.
- Questions diligence asks:
  - What are the **implications** of working with this AI?
  - **Who might be affected** by what is created, by the collaboration, or by *missed inaccuracies*?
  - **Who has access to the data** used to produce this output?
  - How do I ensure the interaction and outcome **align with ethical standards and values**?
- **Analogy: driving a car.** We don't only focus on getting from A to B efficiently. We also consider **safety, rules, and how we affect others**. AI interactions *don't exist in a vacuum*.

### 1. Creation Diligence
- **Creation diligence** is the ability to be **critical and intentional about which AI systems you work with and how you work with them**.
- Questions to ask:
  - **How is this system trained and built?** What data was used?
  - **Who owns the data I'm inputting**, and who may have access once it's shared?
  - How am I protecting the **privacy and security** of myself and others?
  - What **other impacts** does this system have?
  - Does this align with **my values** and **my organization's policies**?
- Example: before sharing **sensitive company information** with an AI, check whether the service has **appropriate data protection policies** and whether your **organization permits it**.

### 2. Transparency Diligence
- **Transparency diligence** is the ability to be **open and accurate about AI interaction with everyone who needs to know**.
- Different settings (**personal, academic, creative, professional**) have *different expectations of disclosure*. The responsibility is on **each of us to understand and meet them**.
- Questions to ask:
  - **Who needs to know** about AI's role in this work?
  - **How and when** should I communicate it?
  - **What level of detail** makes sense?
- Transparency is not just about rules. It's about **maintaining trust and respect**. People have the **right to know** when AI played a significant role in content or in decisions that affect them.
- Example: when AI helps draft a **team proposal**, telling colleagues **which parts were AI-assisted** allows *more honest collaboration*.

### 3. Deployment Diligence
- **Deployment diligence** is the ability to **take informed responsibility for outputs you use or share** after they've been created with AI.
- AI makes mistakes. When you share AI-generated content, **you, not the AI, are ultimately responsible** for its **accuracy and appropriateness**.
- That means:
  - **Verifying facts**
  - **Checking for biases**
  - Ensuring **accuracy and usage rights**
  - Doing the checks needed so you **can stand behind what you share**
- Example: a **journalist** using AI to draft an article must **verify every fact and source** and meet *the same standards as if they'd written it entirely themselves*.

### Navigating Diligence
- It isn't always straightforward, because **contexts and stakeholders differ** in expectations and standards.
- **Develop personal guidelines** for working with AI that align with *your own ethics and values*.
- In professional settings, learn **organizational policies and industry standards**.
- **Legal and regulatory frameworks** for AI are still emerging, so **staying informed** is part of diligence.

### Recap
- **Creation, transparency, and deployment diligence** together form the Diligence competency.
- Diligence means being **thoughtful about the systems we choose**, **honest about AI's role** in our work, and **accountable for what we create**.
- Our own behaviors play a key role in making AI **fair, safe, and beneficial to society**.

### Key Takeaways

- **Diligence** is about taking **responsibility for our AI collaborations**.
- **Creation Diligence** involves being thoughtful about **which AI systems we use and how we engage with them**.
- **Transparency Diligence** means being **honest about AI's role** in our work with everyone who needs to know.
- **Deployment Diligence** requires taking responsibility for **verifying and vouching for the outputs** we use or share.
- **Different contexts** (personal, academic, professional) may have different expectations for **disclosure and verification**.
- Thoughtful diligence helps ensure our AI collaborations are not only **effective and efficient**, but also **ethical and safe**.

## Exercise: Creating a Diligence Statement

### What Is a Diligence Statement?
- A **transparent acknowledgment of AI's role** in your work, plus your **commitment to responsibility for the final output**.

### Template
> In creating this **[document/project/content]**, I collaborated with **[AI assistant name]** to assist with **[specific tasks: drafting, research, editing, etc.]**. I affirm that all **AI-generated and co-created content underwent thorough review and evaluation**. The final output **accurately reflects my understanding, expertise, and intended meaning**. While AI assistance was instrumental in the process, I **maintain full responsibility** for the content, its accuracy, and its presentation. This disclosure is made in the spirit of **transparency** and to acknowledge the role of AI in the creation process.

### Reflect Before Drafting
- **Creation diligence:**
  - Which **AI systems** did I choose, and why?
  - What **data or information** did I share with the AI?
  - Were there **privacy, security, or ethical** considerations?
- **Transparency diligence:**
  - Who is the **audience**, and what do they expect about **AI disclosure**?
  - How specifically did **AI contribute** to different parts of the work?
- **Deployment diligence:**
  - What steps did I take to **verify accuracy and appropriateness**?
  - How did I make sure the output **meets my standards**?
  - What **responsibility** am I taking for the final product?

### Draft With Claude
- Share your **reflections** (and optionally past conversations about the project).
- Make sure the statement covers:
  - **Which AI systems** you used
  - **How the AI contributed**
  - Your **review process**
  - Your **assertion of responsibility**
  - Any **context-specific considerations** (academic, professional, etc.)
- Add it to the project in a suitable place (**footer, appendix, or metadata**).

## Reflection

### Which aspect of Diligence do I find most challenging, and why?
- **Deployment diligence.** Verifying every fact and claim *takes real time*, and it's tempting to trust **fluent, confident** output.
- **Transparency diligence** is close behind, because *expectations about disclosure aren't always clear or consistent*.

### How might my approach vary by context?
- **Personal:** **lighter disclosure**, but I still protect **private data**.
- **Academic:** follow the **institution's AI policy**, *cite or disclose AI use*, and make sure the work shows **my own understanding**.
- **Professional:** follow **company policy**, never share **confidential or client data** without approval, and *be explicit with colleagues and clients about AI's role*.
- **Creative:** consider **credit, originality, and audience expectations**.

### How does acknowledging AI's role affect how others perceive my work?
- It can **build trust** by showing I'm *honest and accountable*.
- Some audiences may **question the work's originality**, so I need to be clear about **what I contributed** and **how I reviewed the result**.
- Being specific (*what AI did, what I did, how I checked it*) is more credible than a vague disclaimer.

### What ethical considerations did I not anticipate?
- *(Fill in after the project.)* Possible examples: **what data I pasted into prompts**, **bias in the output**, **usage rights for generated content**, **how much of the thinking was really mine**.

### What personal guidelines might I develop?
- **Check data policies** before sharing anything sensitive, and **never paste confidential or personal data** by default.
- **Verify every important fact, number, and source** before sharing.
- **Disclose AI's role** wherever the audience would expect it.
- **Keep final judgment and ownership with me**: *"The AI said so" is never a defense*.
- **Stay informed** about changing policies, laws, and best practices.
- **Review these guidelines regularly** as the technology changes.
