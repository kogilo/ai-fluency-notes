## Introduction to AI Fluency

- The course is about **how humans interact and collaborate with AI systems**, imagining AI as a _trusted partner_ for **creative and innovative problem-solving**, and approaching it **confidently and capably**.
- Goal: engage with AI in ways that are **effective, efficient, ethical, and safe**, which means _fundamentally shifting how we approach AI_.
- **The AI Fluency Framework** is a model for human-AI interaction built on **four core competencies, the 4 Ds**:
  - **Delegation:** when should humans do the work, and when should AI?
  - **Description:** how do we communicate clearly with AI systems?
  - **Discernment:** how do we evaluate what AI gives us?
  - **Diligence:** how do we ensure our AI interaction is _responsible, transparent, and accountable_?
- Each section pairs **focused videos with exercises and materials**. _Practice what you learn._
- Most AI training focuses on **tactical skills** (specific prompts, settings, tools) that **quickly become outdated**. This course focuses on **core competencies and deep understanding** to prepare for _future changes in AI_, not just today's.
- **By the end, you'll have:**
  - A **framework** to guide your own AI interactions
  - **Confidence** in deciding _when and how_ to engage AI effectively
  - **Practical skills** for more thoughtful, fluid human-AI collaboration
  - The ability to **evaluate and take responsibility** for the outcomes

  ## What Is AI Fluency?

- **AI is reshaping** how we communicate, create, learn, and solve problems, but _having access to AI doesn't mean we know how to use it well or responsibly_. There is a gap between **having access** and being **truly fluent**.
- **AI fluency** is not about being a technical expert or memorizing the "10 best prompts." It's a collection of **practical skills, knowledge, insights, and values** that reinforce each other and _adapt as the technology changes_.
- At its heart, AI fluency means interacting with AI in ways that are **effective, efficient, ethical, and safe**: getting the most out of AI _without wasting time and energy_, and doing it _honestly and responsibly_, protecting the **privacy and security** of yourself and others.

### Three Ways We Engage with AI

- **Automation:** AI completes a **specific task** based on your instructions (summarize a document, draft an email, create an image, plan a trip).
  - You define _what_ needs to be done, and the AI executes it.
  - Works well with a **clear outcome in mind**, but is harder when you're _not sure what you're looking for_.
- **Augmentation:** you and the AI **collaborate on a task together**. The AI is a _creative thinking and problem-solving partner_, not a machine.
  - Examples: developing a story character, working through an architectural problem in an app, shaping thoughts on a research topic.
  - The AI _doesn't do the work for you, but helps you do your work better_.
  - Best when **solutions aren't straightforward** and you need space to explore and experiment.
- **Agency:** AI **works independently on your behalf**.
  - Examples: categorizing and drafting replies to incoming emails, an ever-changing interactive website experience, a dynamic character in a game.
  - You establish the AI's **knowledge and behavior patterns** rather than specific actions, _less like a script writer, more like a director setting a vision_.

### Key Takeaways

- **No mode is inherently better**. Each serves different purposes, and you might use **all three in a single project**.
- Many people start with automation, but **augmentation and agency** take the most advantage of AI's unique capabilities and often lead to the **most creative and effective solutions**.
- AI is **more than a tool**. It can be a **tool, a medium, a partner, or a co-creator**, sometimes all at once. This shift means we have **new roles** and need to **develop new skills**.
- Those skills make up the **AI Fluency Framework**: **four key areas of competence** to develop and master, whichever way you engage with AI.

### The 4D Framework

- Whether you work with AI through **automation, augmentation, or agency**, there are **four essential competencies** for collaborating _effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely_: the **four Ds**.

#### 1. Delegation: _the big picture_

- Ask: **What are you trying to accomplish? What work should you handle yourself? Where might AI help?**
- Example: let AI **review lengthy documents and data** and discuss implications, but **reserve critical analysis and final conclusions for yourself**.
- To delegate effectively:
  - Understand your **goal and the problem** you're solving
  - Recognize what AI **can and can't do well**
  - **Thoughtfully divide the work** between you and the AI
- Delegation is **not just offloading tasks**. It's having a **clear vision** and _strategically choosing how AI fits into your process_.
- Essential for **effective and efficient** collaboration.

#### 2. Description: _clear communication with AI_

- Compare a vague "make me a logo" with describing your **company values, target audience, preferred colors, and style references**.
- Example: as a tutor, tell the AI _"don't tell me the answer, just help me work through this step by step."_
- It goes **beyond writing prompts**. It means **detailed, context-rich conversations** that set out:
  - The **goal and output format**
  - How the AI should **approach the task**
  - The **context and information** it needs
  - The **tone and style** of interaction
- Effective description _sets up both you and the AI for collaborative success_.

#### 3. Discernment: _evaluating what AI gives you_

- Example: AI suggests a marketing strategy. Ask: **Are the facts accurate? Does the reasoning make sense? Does it align with your brand and audience? Does it help you move forward?**
- Draws on **your own domain expertise** and requires **judgment and critical insight** to separate _what's useful from what's not_, and to know when to **refine or set aside** an output.
- Most AI interactions are **small loops of description and discernment**: _describe, evaluate, refine, repeat_.

#### 4. Diligence: _responsible AI interaction_

- Key questions:
  - Are you ensuring **fairness and controlling for bias**? (e.g., job descriptions, application reviews)
  - Are you **verifying accuracy** for important decisions?
  - Are you **protecting sensitive data**?
  - Are you **transparent** about AI's involvement?
  - Are you **accountable** for AI-assisted work?
- Diligence means **taking ownership** and being willing to **stand behind** final AI-assisted products.
- Critical for **safe and ethical** collaboration.

### Recap

- **AI fluency** = practical **skills, knowledge, insights, and values** for using AI _effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely_.
- The 4 Ds in short:
  - **Delegation:** decide _when and how_ to use AI
  - **Description:** _communicate clearly_ with AI
  - **Discernment:** _evaluate_ AI outputs
  - **Diligence:** use AI _responsibly_
- These competencies are **not tied to specific tools or techniques** that may become outdated. They're **fundamental skills** that help you _adapt and grow alongside_ a rapidly evolving technology.
